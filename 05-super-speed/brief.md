# Give quiet responders a way back

**For:** Helen Achebe, Director of Product  
**Experience:** Linda Pruitt, handler, and Farlight, responder  
**Status:** Proposal for testing; no production changes approved or implemented.

## The problem

Linda needs to understand why Farlight has stopped receiving callouts and what can happen next. Farlight’s weekly pings fell from 12 in the week starting 3 August to 3, 1 and 0 in the weeks starting 17, 24 and 31 August. Silence leaves the handler without an explanation and the responder without a clear route back.

Dispatch currently treats a missed ping like a deliberate turn-down when updating acceptance history. Taking another callout is the only implemented way to rebuild an existing score. That creates a possible trap when opportunities become scarce. The supplied code supports this mechanism, but historical rankings and phone-display times are unavailable: we have not established how much it explains Farlight’s decline.

## What changes for Linda and Farlight

**More time to answer—separate test.** Restoring the ping wait from 60 to 90 seconds remains a proposal for separate evaluation. Keep the wait at 60 seconds during the initial recovery-only test. Prototype an expiry explanation: “Missed—the callout moved on.” A system wait is not a promise of the same duration after the phone displays the offer; delivery timing needs verification.

**Less lasting harm from a miss.** Keep the immediate history-score penalty at 0.12 for both missed pings and deliberate turn-downs, and test automatic recovery for misses alone. This supersedes the earlier proposal to halve the miss penalty. Keep proximity weighting intact. The experiment tests whether making miss penalties temporary restores opportunities, without changing their initial size or asking Linda to tune routing settings.

**A route back that does not require an offer first.** Automatically recover penalties attributable to missed pings only, without requiring another taken callout. Each miss has an independent schedule: recovery begins 24 hours after that miss, then removes its actual deduction at an even, time-based rate over the next 72 hours, completing 96 hours after the miss. A later miss adds its own penalty and schedule without resetting earlier recovery. Deliberate turn-down penalties do not receive automatic recovery; the existing credit for taking a callout remains. This policy and timing are selected for testing, not validated findings. Automatic recovery restores at most the points actually deducted for each miss, subject to the existing 1.0 score ceiling; it does not stop at the neutral score of 0.50. For example, a miss that reduces a score from 0.03 to 0 can restore at most 0.03. Verified older miss deductions qualify for the same recovery schedule, measured from the original miss time. Older losses whose actual deduction or timing cannot be verified remain unchanged and are flagged for review. A missed-ping count alone is insufficient to establish an actual score deduction. Wen and Marcus must verify the historical records before a pilot. For an actual deduction D, cumulative scheduled recovery at elapsed time t hours is D × min(1, max(0, (t − 24) / 72)); apply only recovery not previously processed. A full 0.12 deduction therefore schedules 0.04 recovery per 24 hours during the recovery window. When the score reaches 1.0, cancel all remaining recovery from earlier misses; no unused recovery is banked against future penalties. This applies whether acceptance credits or recovery bring the score to 1.0. Historical replay must respect these cancellation events. This restores consideration; it does not guarantee a callout or a place at the top of the list.

**Expired offers and late taps.** Accept a response only if it reaches the server strictly before the deadline and the offer is still active. A response at or after the deadline cannot take the callout; show “This offer has expired.” Check the deadline and active-offer state atomically with assignment so a late or duplicate response cannot reclaim or double-assign a callout. No grace period. For the recovery-only pilot, retain the supplied code’s start event: the server deadline is 60 seconds after the send function returns. Record send, display and response times where available, distinguishing unavailable timestamps from confirmed events. Delivery delays can shorten visible response time; do not claim the responder receives 60 on-screen seconds. If connectivity drops after a Take tap but before confirmation, show “Confirming—assignment not yet confirmed.” Reconcile with the server when connectivity returns. Show “Assigned” only after server confirmation, or “This offer has expired” when the server confirms expiry. Loss of connectivity alone proves neither outcome; the original server deadline still applies.

**A useful explanation for Linda.** Show the last 7 days versus the previous 7 on Farlight’s coverage card, with total pings and separate taken, turned-down and missed counts for each period. Show “No pings” for a verified zero count and “Data unavailable” when records cannot be loaded. This comparison describes activity, not the cause of a change. Use plain-language recovery status and timing, without a numeric history score: “Recovery starts [time],” “Missed-ping penalties are recovering,” or “No recovery pending,” with timing details for each miss. Show “Older losses need review” separately where applicable. If recovery records cannot be loaded, show “Recovery data unavailable” rather than implying no recovery is pending. Explain that recovery does not guarantee a callout. Linda can review what happened and explain the next step. If the reason for fewer pings is unknown, say so instead of presenting a guessed ranking explanation.

## What stays in place

Preserve the increased emphasis on proximity, console filter persistence and unrelated release fixes. Continue offering callouts sequentially and recording misses separately from turn-downs. No handler-adjustable ranking control, guaranteed allocation, or automatic availability change based on quiet activity. This proposal does not revert all of 4.2.

## Test before shipping

First, walk Linda through a prototype: inspect Farlight’s quiet stretch, explain an expired ping, and describe recovery without promising work. Test the responder flow with Farlight, including whether the expiry message and recovery explanation are clear.

Run recovery calculations in shadow mode first: compute proposed scores alongside current scores without changing routing or offers. Wen and Marcus review calculation correctness and logging before a limited live pilot. Shadow mode cannot establish that responders will receive more opportunities. Both stages isolate recovery: retain the 60-second wait, 0.12 penalties and existing routing weights. Select a small live-pilot cohort from the handlers and responders whose quiet stretches surfaced in post-4.2 feedback, applying consistent eligibility criteria: reduced offers, recorded misses and verifiable deductions. Candidate pairs are Linda Pruitt / Farlight, Desmond Okafor / The Undertow, Yusuf Demir / Corporal Ashgrove and Simone Fischer / Halfmoon (support tickets), plus Kip / Meteor Mite and Aunt Dot / Vesper (interviews). These are candidates, not confirmed eligible participants; the 4.2 release page itself does not name this cohort. Wen and Ravi must define numeric eligibility thresholds, verify records and confirm the list before launch. Monitor responders competing for the same callouts as well; they are affected by the change and are not clean controls. Run the limited live pilot for an initial 14 days, with daily monitoring and a day-7 review. If eligible callout volume is insufficient, report an inconclusive result and review before extending; do not declare success or extend automatically. Before launch, Ravi must propose baseline-derived numeric cohort eligibility criteria, a minimum meaningful improvement in pings per available hour, minimum observation volume, and a maximum rate of callouts with no recorded acceptance. These thresholds and the assignment-delay guardrails require PM approval before launch; do not choose thresholds after seeing pilot results. Insufficient evidence remains inconclusive. Evaluate a 90-second wait separately afterward. A scenario check shows that three fully unanswered offers take 270 seconds at a 90-second wait versus 180 seconds today. Include verified older miss deductions under the same schedule; leave unverifiable losses unchanged pending review. The historical adjustment must account for subsequent score changes and prevent duplicate restoration before it can be implemented. These are mechanics checks, not evidence of real-world improvement.

The primary success measure is pings per available hour for previously quiet responders, examined responder by responder alongside demand and capability match. Collect reliable availability histories before evaluating this measure; aggregate ping acceptance alone cannot establish success. Ravi should track callouts with no recorded acceptance and time from incident creation to acceptance as guardrails. Track ping acceptance and misses separately from deliberate turn-downs; an improving aggregate can hide responders receiving fewer opportunities. Before launch, Marcus and Ravi must establish baseline assignment timing and propose explicit limits for median and 90th-percentile time from incident creation to acceptance (the latter marks the slowest 10% boundary). The PM must approve these evidence-based limits before the live pilot can start. Define measurement windows and minimum sample sizes with the limits; do not invent a numeric cutoff without baseline evidence. On a breach of an approved guardrail, automatically stop applying recovery to routing and revert to a validated no-recovery score state while Marcus investigates. Preserve existing assignments and audit records. Before launch, Wen must demonstrate rollback that accounts for intervening takes and turn-downs; merely disabling future recovery leaves restored points in place and is insufficient. Rollback does not undo offers or other operational consequences that have already occurred.

**Next step:** Build and verify the prototype and shadow calculation against the selected policies and [engineering acceptance criteria](dispatch-recovery-acceptance-criteria.md). Return with baseline-derived thresholds, validated historical treatment and rollback evidence for approval before a live pilot. These product decisions do not authorize production deployment.
